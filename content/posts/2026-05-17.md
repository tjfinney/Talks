## In the beginning God

The first reading for today is the Hebrew account of creation. It begins like this:

> בְּרֵאשִׁ֖ית בָּרָ֣א אֱלֹהִ֑ים אֵ֥ת הַשָּׁמַ֖יִם וְאֵ֥ת הָאָֽרֶץ׃

> וְהָאָ֗רֶץ הָיְתָ֥ה תֹ֨הוּ֙ וָבֹ֔הוּ וְחֹ֖שֶׁךְ עַל־פְּנֵ֣י תְהֹ֑ום וְר֣וּחַ אֱלֹהִ֔ים מְרַחֶ֖פֶת עַל־פְּנֵ֥י הַמָּֽיִם׃ 

> וַיֹּ֥אמֶר אֱלֹהִ֖ים יְהִ֣י אֹ֑ור וַֽיְהִי־אֹֽור׃

[Gen 1.1-3 Hebrew Bible](https://marble.bible/text?s=undefined&dictid=000000&db=Hebrew&verse=1&textid=00100100200008&book=Genesis&chapter=1)

> Bereshith bara Elohim et ha shamayim ve'et ha'aretz

> Veha'aretz hayetah tohu vavohu ve'choshech al-penei tehom veruach Elohim merachefet al-penei hamayim

> Vayomer Elohim yehi or vayhi or

Sorry for my sorry attempt to pronounce it. Here is how the Jerusalem Bible translates it:

> In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.

> The earth was a formless void, there was darkness over the deep, and God's spirit hovered over the water.

> God said, 'Let there be light', and there was light.

In English you add *s* to a word to make it plural. In Hebrew you add *im*, and the word for God used here has the plural ending: *Elohim*. It seems that there is some aspect of plurality in the Creator: He is already there when creation begins, his spirit is there hovering over the water like a bird hovering over its young. His speech is there too, and what he says happens.

The [Septuagint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septuagint) is a translation of the Hebrew Bible into [Koine Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koine_Greek) begun about 250 BC. *Septuagint* means *seventy*. Tradition says that seventy Hebrews did the translation. People refer to it using the abbreviation *LXX*, which is *seventy* in Roman numerals. Here is the beginning of Genesis in the Septuagint:

> ἐν ἀρχῇ ἐποίησεν ὁ θεὸς τὸν οὐρανὸν καὶ τὴν γῆν

[Gen 1.1 LXX](https://www.crosswire.org/study/parallelstudy.jsp?del=all&add=KJV&add=WLC&add=NASB&add=LXX)

John's Gospel begins with the same two words (ην has smooth breathing):

> εν αρχη ην ο λογος και ο λογος ην προς τον θεον και θεος ην ο λογος

[John 1.1 Greek New Testament](https://www.crosswire.org/study/parallelstudy.jsp?key=John+1%3A1#cv)

In the beginning was the *logos*, and the *logos* was with God, and the *logos* *was* God.

What does that *logos* word mean? Here's a definition from Barclay Newman's *Concise Greek-English Dictionary of the New Testament*:

> something said (e.g. word; saying; message; teaching; talk, conversation; ... preaching); Word (in Johannine Christology); account, settlement of an account; value; reason, grounds; matter, thing; book.

As you can see, *logos* is a loaded word. According to John, God's Word was with God in the beginning, and in fact *is* God. As much as your speech is you, God's speech is God. John's message to us is that this Divine Reason for Being, God's voice through whom all things were created, became a human being and pitched his tent with us. We read about the Word of God in the Word of God: one is divine words transmitted to us through the medium of print; the other is a life giving spirit. He is now seated at God's right hand in heaven. He is the Way, the Truth, the Life. He is the Gate. He is the light of the world. He is the bread of heaven. He is the Good Shepherd. He is the Head of the Church. And He is our Advocate.

The Spirit was also with God in the beginning. In Hebrew the word is *ruach* and in Greek it is *pneuma*. Both words can mean *spirit*, or *breath*, or *wind*. Just as the Word is God, so is the Spirit. God the Father, God the Son, and God the Holy Spirit are as closely connected as your voice is connected to your breath. The Spirit hovers over creation like a bird protecting its young. The Spirit was with God in the beginning, an essence that is essential to all that God does. The Spirit came down like a bird and settled on Jesus when he was baptized by John.

Early Christian theologians came up with a special word to describe the union of Father, Son, and Spirit. [Perichoresis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perichoresis) means something like *co-inherence*. Augustine describes the union like this:

> In that highest Trinity one is as much as the three together, nor are two anything more than one. And They are infinite in themselves. So both each are in each, and all in each, and each in all, and all in all, and all are one. 

## Back to Genesis

Getting back to Genesis,

* God speaks to separate light from darkness: there is evening and morning, the first day. 

* God speaks to separate waters above from waters below: there is evening and morning, the second day. 

* God speaks to separate sea and land, telling the earth to produce plants: there is evening and morning, the third day.

* God speaks to make lights appear in the sky and shine on the earth: there is evening and morning, the fourth day.

* God speaks to make the sea teem with life the air to be filled with things that fly: there is evening and morning, the fifth day.

* God speaks to make life fill the land. Then God says, *Let us make humanity in our own image. And so God creates humanity, male and female he creates them. He blesses them and gives them the job of looking after his creation. There is evening and morning, the sixth day.

That is how heaven and earth were created with all their array. On the seventh day God completed his work. He blessed the seventh day and made it holy because on that day he rested from his creative work. That's where we get the Sabbath from. *Seven* in Hebrew is *Shabbat*. It's a good and God-given thing to rest on the seventh day.

## That's wrong isn't it?

So that's how the heavens and earth were made. But isn't that all wrong? Wasn't there a big bang that made all the hydrogen and a bit of helium? Wasn't there a first generation of stars that made the elements up to iron? Weren't there neutron star collisions and other cosmic conflagrations to make everything heavier than iron, including gold and uranium? Isn't that what our solar system formed from? Didn't it take thousands of millions of years to happen, rather than seven days? What about the sun, moon, and stars appearing after sea, sky, and land. Isn't this just a bronze age story with no relation to what really happened?

Recently I attended a [great presentation](https://iscast.org/events/iscast-perth-has-science-accidentally-found-evidence-there-is-a-god/) by Martin Hill, Margaret and Brian's son. If you ever get a chance to see one of his presentations, I recommend it. His talk was titled *Has science accidentally found evidence there is a God.* He presented an array of recent scientific findings that are consistent with the Genesis account, including that the early earth was a dark water world. What frame of reference you use is always important in science. If your frame of reference is between the water below and the thick condensate above then the sun, moon, and stars do appear after the land and sea separate. As for the seven days, if I start talking about what happened *in my day*, you know I'm talking about an undefined expanse of time, as will be the length of today's message.

There is another aspect of the Genesis account that is worth considering. That is its poetic nature. The first day is about light and darkness. The second day is about sea and sky. The third day is about land and plants. The fourth day corresponds to the first day: it relates to lights in the sky. The fifth day parallels the second day: it has to do with fish in the sea and birds in the sky. The sixth day lines up with the third day: it talks about things that live on the land and humans to look after the land.

The poetic structure serves to emphasize the seventh day. This is a special day with no parallel, though it does hark back to the very beginning where all that was *was* God. God, who was already there before He spoke what we see into being, blesses the seventh day and makes it holy. It is a day set apart for resting from work. God tells us to take a day off and blesses it to make it holy.

## What is humanity?

Mark tells us that Jesus said this about the Sabbath:

> The Sabbath came into being for the benefit of people, it wasn't people who came into being for the benefit of the Sabbath. That's why Humanity's Son is Lord even of the Sabbath.

[Mark 2.27-28 USC]

This is from Richard K. Moore's translation of the New Testament into Australian English. Notice that he says *Humanity's Son* instead of the usual *Son of Man*. He does this because he wants to give us the best chance of understanding the message as the original hearers would have understood it.

What's so special about humanity, anyway. That brings us to the second reading for today, Psalm 8. It turns out that this Psalm has a chiastic structure.

Have you ever heard of chiastic structure? I hear a collective groan. If you haven't suffered through this before, chiastic structure is where the beginning corresponds to the end, the second thing corresponds to the second last thing, and so on until the middle. In chiastic structure the thing in the middle is emphasized.

In Psalm 8 the beginning and the end are exactly the same:

> LORD, our Lord, how great your name throughout the earth!

[Quotes are from Psalm 8, Jerusalem Bible]

The second and second-last parts are about God's creation, the heavens above and the earth below. Concerning the heavens above it says,

> Above the heavens is your majesty chanted by the mouths of children, babes in arms. You set your stronghold firm against your foes to subdue enemies and rebels.

In Matthew's Gospel it says,

> At the sight of the wonderful things he did and of the children shouting, 'Hosanna to the Son of David' in the Temple, the chief priests and the scribes were indignant. 'Do you hear what they are saying?' they said to him. 'Yes,' Jesus answered 'have you never read this:

> By the mouths of children, babes in arms, you have made sure of praise?

[Matt 21.15-16]

Psalm 8 continues on:

> I look up at your heavens, made by your fingers, at the moon and the stars you set in place--

> ah, what is man that you should spare a thought for him, the son of man that you should care for him?

This is the centre, the part emphasized by the poetic structure. What is humanity, that you should spare a thought? What a good question.

Have you ever heard of the [Pale Blue Dot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale_Blue_Dot)? According to Wikipedia,

> Pale Blue Dot is a photograph of Earth taken on February 14, 1990, by the Voyager 1 space probe from an unprecedented distance of over 6 billion kilometers (3.7 billion miles, 40.5 AU), as part of that day's Family Portrait series of images of the Solar System. In the photograph, Earth's apparent size is less than a pixel; the planet appears as a tiny dot against the vastness of space, among bands of sunlight reflected by the camera. Commissioned by NASA and resulting from the advocacy of astronomer and author Carl Sagan, the photograph was interpreted in Sagan's 1994 book, *Pale Blue Dot*, as representing humanity's minuscule and ephemeral place amidst the cosmos.

## The Infinite is made Finite

What is humanity that God should pay any attention? I don't know, but here is something worth paying attention to:

> He became what we are that He might make us what He is.

[Athanasius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athanasius_of_Alexandria)

Athanasius said that. He was the Patriarch of Alexandria in the fourth century. In 367 AD he sent out an Easter Letter that included a list of the 27 books we now accept as the New Testament. There is a saying, *Athanasius contra mundum*, which means *Athanasius against the world.* Athanasius held that Jesus is fully God, unlike someone named Arius who said *There was a time when he was not.* That's how the Arian controversy started. Athanasius wouldn't budge. He was exiled over and over again for saying that Jesus is fully God. In the end he prevailed. The Apostle's Creed goes back to this time. It says that God the Father, God the Son, and God the Holy Spirit are all God. They are co-inherent. They are co-eternal.

## Infinite to finite and vice versa

What is humanity that God should care? Humanity was made god-like in the beginning and is also god-like because God became one of us. Not like us, but fully one of us. Jesus Christ is fully divine and fully human. How can that be? How could the infinite God become a finite human being? And how could that finite human being become infinite again, rising from the dead with the power of an everlasting life?

Now we are fully into the mystery of the incarnation, something Athanasius wrote a book about. I don't have any answers. Instead I'll finish with a bit of modern physics.

## Making the infinite finite

Modern physics has a bit of a problem: sometimes its equations go to infinity. There's a problem when your equation blows up and gives an answer of infinity instead of what you have just measured and found to be finite. Paul Dirac came up with an equation like that. Here's what Wikipedia says:

> [Dirac's] explanation of spin as a consequence of the union of quantum mechanics and relativity—and the eventual discovery of the positron—represents one of the great triumphs of theoretical physics. This accomplishment has been described as fully on par with the works of Isaac Newton, James Clerk Maxwell, and Albert Einstein before him. The equation has been deemed by some physicists to be "the real seed of modern physics". The Dirac equation has been described as the "centerpiece of relativistic quantum mechanics", and as "perhaps the most important [equation] in all of quantum mechanics".

[Wikipedia, Dirac equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_equation)

To get around the problem, physicists do something called [renormalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renormalization). Dirac was a pioneer of the technique. However he didn't like it and said the following:

> [Renormalization is] just a stop-gap procedure. There must be some fundamental change in our ideas, probably a change just as fundamental as the passage from Bohr’s orbit theory to quantum mechanics. When you get a number turning out to be infinite which ought to be finite, you should admit that there is something wrong with your equations, and not hope that you can get a good theory just by doctoring up that number.

[Chris Oakley, *The search for a quantum field theory*](http://www.cgoakley.org/qft/)

Here's an example of the kind of sums they do. What do you get if you add one plus two plus three and keep going forever? Infinity? Some would say that the answer is [negative one-twelfth](https://youtu.be/w-I6XTVZXww?si=ZcULX7chYjqMtvQZ). What?!! Well, look it up on YouTube. Type in "the sum of all positive integers" and you'll find it.

What's this got to do with the Triune God? My answer is that God is incomprehensible. Trying to understand God's thoughts and the way God made everything is a bit like ants trying to understand our thoughts. Yet God understands and has become comprehensible by becoming human. As Athanasius said,

> He became what we are that He might make us what He is.

Amen
