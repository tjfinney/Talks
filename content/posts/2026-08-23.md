## The burning bush

When Moses met God he was in trouble. He had been someone once: he was rescued from the Nile by Pharoah's daughter. Everything was going great until at the age of forty he had an identity crisis. Even though he was like a prince he was not the real thing. In truth he was a Hebrew, and his people were slaves in Egypt. One day when he saw an Egyptian boss abusing one of his people he hit the boss a bit too hard and killed him. That's how he got into trouble.

It might surprise you to know that when Moses saw the burning bush he was eighty. (See [Exodus 7.7](https://www.crosswire.org/study/parallelstudy.jsp?key=Exodus+7%3A7#cv).) It was forty years since he took off from Egypt to get away from Pharoah. He had been looking after sheep in the middle of nowhere for a really long time and probably had regrets: *If only I'd stayed home that day and not bothered with the Hebrews. Life would have been great! Nice place to live. Plenty to eat. Nothing to worry about. I used to be like a prince. Now I'm looking after someone else's sheep.*

That's when God's Angel showed up. A bush looked like it was on fire but it wasn't burning down. Moses thought that was worth a look and was still a way off when a voice calls out, *Moses! Moses!!* The Hebrew word there means *shout*, not *whisper*. The voice shouted like you do when calling out to someone who is a way off.

## God's Angel

This isn't the first time God's Angel appears in the Bible. Does anyone want to hazard a guess about when the first time was? Or, more specifically, who was the first person God's Angel talked to in the Bible? The answer is Rahab, Sarah's slave girl. 

God's Angel has a special name. The Hebrews write down what we translate as *the Angel of the LORD* but they never say the part that contains God's ineffable name, the tetragrammaton, what we write down as *LORD*, all in capital letters. *Ineffable* means *incapable of being expressed in words; unspeakable; unutterable; indescribable*. The Hebrew has the word for angel, *malach*, then the tetragrammaton, the four letters *yod*, *he*, *vav*, *hey*. However, when the Hebrews say *God's Angel* they say *Malach Adonai*. Whenever the Hebrews see the ineffable name written down they always substitute *Adonai*, as in *Lord*, or *ha Shem*, as in *the Name*. Why? Because a Hebrew does not want to break the third of the ten commandments, which says:

> You shall not misuse the name of [the LORD] your God, for [the LORD] will not leave unpunished anyone who misuses his name.

[Ex 20.7 NJB](https://www.crosswire.org/study/parallelstudy.jsp?key=Exodus+20%3A7#cv)

The Hebrew word *sheva* is the one translated as *misuse* here. *Sheva* is the name for one of the Hebrew pointings, which act as vowels. It indicates a sound with no content, like what goes between the first *b* and *n* in *banana*. We spell it *a* but say it as if it's not there. The third commandment is saying not to use God's name in that way. The way the Hebrews make sure of this is to *never* say the name: they say *Adonai* or *ha Shem* instead.

*Malach* has a couple of meanings. It can mean *angel* or *messenger*. Greek is the same: *angelos* means *angel* or *messenger*. So *Malach Adonai* can mean *God's Angel* or *God's Messenger*.

## A certain pattern

If you look at places where God's Angel appears there is a certain pattern:

* the Angel appears to a person who is in serious trouble
* the Angel speaks to that person and they have a straightforward conversation
* there is talk of holiness, that is, things being dedicated to God
* the person gets scared because he or she has a growing realisation that they are actually talking to God
* there is talk of seeing, hearing, and knowing
* the person is given a promise
* the person is sent out, often to do an impossible job 
* there is talk of names
* the person ends up surprised to still be alive after seeing God.

You don't always see each one of these elements but each one is often there. Let's look at a few of the times God's Angel appears, starting with when he appeared to Hagar, then Jacob at the Jabbok, Gideon in the wine press, and Samson's mother near an oak tree.

## Hagar at the spring on the road to Shur

Hagar is in trouble because she is a surrogate mother and a runaway slave. Back then the child would be the legal property of the slave owner and the law of the land punished slaves who ran away.

> 7 The angel of [the LORD] found her by a spring in the desert, the spring on the road to Shur.

> 8 He said, 'Hagar, slave-girl of Sarai, where have you come from, and where are you going?' 'I am running away from my mistress Sarai,' she replied.

> 9 The angel of [the LORD] said to her, 'Go back to your mistress and submit to her.'

> 10 The angel of [the LORD] further said to her, 'I shall make your descendants too numerous to be counted.'

> 11 Then the angel of [the LORD] said to her: Now, you have conceived and will bear a son, and you shall name him Ishmael, for [the LORD] has heard your cries of distress.

> 12 A wild donkey of a man he will be, his hand against every man, and every man's hand against him, living his life in defiance of all his kinsmen.

> 13 Hagar gave a name to [the LORD] who had spoken to her, 'You are El Roi,' by which she meant, 'Did I not go on seeing here, after him who sees me?'

> 14 This is why the well is called the well of Lahai Roi; it is between Kadesh and Bered.

[Gen 16.7-13 NJB](https://www.crosswire.org/study/parallelstudy.jsp?key=Genesis+16%3A7#cv)

There is a lot here but a few points will do. Firstly, the Angel finds Hagar, not the other way around. The hard job Hagar was given was to go back to Sarah and submit to her, and also to face the prospect of her child growing up as a miserable slave as well. As it turns out, God kept his promise to Hagar. He also set Hagar and Ishmael free, even though that freedom came about by Hagar and Ishmael eventually being kicked out of their home. Ishmael is the father of the Arabs. His brother Isaac is the father of the Hebrews. To this day they defy each other though they are both Abraham's children. We hope that one day they will remember that they are brothers and be reconciled. It will take a wonder working God to do that.

## Jacob at the Jabbok

The next time I'd like to look at is when Jacob was at a stream named the Jabbok. You'll be happy to know that I'm not going to look at every time God's Angel appear, though that would be worth doing.

Jacob was in trouble. He was about to meet Esau who he feared because Jacob had tricked Esau out of his inheritance as the first born son of Isaac. Esau had four hundred men with him. It was evening and Jacob was alone. He was on the other side of the Jabbok to Esau, having just divided his people and flocks into two halves. He thought that if Esau's men fell on one group, maybe the other group might be able to get away.

> 25 And Jacob was left alone. Then someone wrestled with him until daybreak

> 26 who, seeing that he could not master him, struck him on the hip socket, and Jacob's hip was dislocated as he wrestled with him.

> 27 He said, 'Let me go, for day is breaking.' Jacob replied, 'I will not let you go unless you bless me.'

> 28 The other said, 'What is your name?' 'Jacob,' he replied.

> 29 He said, 'No longer are you to be called Jacob, but Israel since you have shown your strength against God and men and have prevailed.'

> 30 Then Jacob asked, 'Please tell me your name.' He replied, 'Why do you ask my name?' With that, he blessed him there.

> 31 Jacob named the place Peniel, 'Because I have seen God face to face,' he said, 'and have survived.'

[Gen 32.25-31 NJB](https://www.crosswire.org/study/parallelstudy.jsp?key=Genesis+32%3A24#cv)

In Hebrew, *Jacob* means *trickster*, *Israel* means *wrestles with God*, and *Peniel* means *God's face*.

## Gideon in a wine-press

Another time was when God's Angel appeared to Gideon. Gideon was in trouble because of the marauding Midianites.

> 11 The Angel of [the LORD] came and sat under the terebinth at Ophrah which belonged to Joash of Abiezer. Gideon his son was threshing wheat inside the wine-press, to keep it hidden from Midian,

> 12 and the Angel of [the LORD] appeared to him and said, '[the LORD] is with you, valiant warrior!'

> 13 Gideon replied, 'Excuse me, my lord, but if [the LORD] is with us, why is all this happening to us? And where are all his miracles which our ancestors used to tell us about when they said, "Did not [the LORD] bring us out of Egypt?" But now [the LORD] has deserted us; he has abandoned us to Midian,'

> 14 At this, [the LORD] turned to him and said, 'Go in this strength of yours, and you will rescue Israel from the power of Midian. Am I not sending you myself?'

> 15 Gideon replied, 'Forgive me, my lord, but how can I deliver Israel? My clan is the weakest in Manasseh and I am the least important of my father's family.'

> 16 [the LORD] replied, 'I shall be with you and you will crush Midian as though it were one man.'

> 17 Gideon said, 'If I have found favour in your sight, give me a sign that you are speaking to me.

> 18 Please do not go away from here until I come back to you, bringing you my offering and laying it before you.' And he replied, 'I shall stay until you come back.'

> 19 Gideon went away, he prepared a young goat and from an ephah of flour he made unleavened cakes. He put the meat into a basket and the broth into a pot, then brought it all to him under the terebinth. As he approached,

> 20 the Angel of [the LORD] said to him, 'Take the meat and unleavened cakes, put them on this rock and pour the broth over them.' Gideon did so.

> 21 The Angel of [the LORD] then stretched out the tip of the staff which he was carrying, and touched the meat and unleavened cakes. Fire sprang from the rock and consumed the meat and unleavened cakes, and the Angel of [the LORD] vanished before his eyes.

> 22 Gideon then knew that this was the Angel of [the LORD], and he said, 'Alas, my Lord [the LORD]! Now I have seen the Angel of [the LORD] face to face!'

> 23 [The LORD] answered, 'Peace be with you; have no fear; you will not die.'

> 24 Gideon built an altar there to [the LORD] and called it [the LORD] is Peace.

[Judges 6.11-24 NJB](https://www.crosswire.org/study/parallelstudy.jsp?key=Judges+6%3A11#cv)

The hard thing Gideon had to do was to defeat Midian even though he was so down-trodden that he had to hide what he was doing from them. Yet he had a commission and a promise from God: *I am sending you* and *I will be with you.* In the Greek translation of the Hebrew Bible, that *sent* word is αποστελλω. That's where we get the word *Apostle* from. When Jesus chose the Apostles he was sending them out on his mission.

Gideon was just like Moses in the sense that God sent him and said *I will be with you*. The hard job Moses had to do was go back and face Pharoah, maybe not the one he had run away from but still a Pharoah of Egypt, where he was wanted for manslaughter.

There is another thing here: In the Hebrew text it was not God's Angel but God himself who turned to Gideon and said *Go in this strength of yours*. Someone who was weak in human terms was nevertheless being sent by the One who is strong. 

## Samson's mother in a field

The last example from the Hebrew Bible I'd like to look at today is Samson's mother. We don't get told her name. Back then it was normal to refer to a woman as a man's wife or daughter because women were treated as nobodies. For instance, *Bathsheba* just means *Sheba's daughter*.

Samson's mother was in trouble because of another group of marauders: this time it was the Philistines.


> 2 There was a man of Zorah of the tribe of Dan, called Manoah. His wife was barren; she had borne no children.

> 3 The Angel of [the LORD] appeared to this woman and said to her, 'You are barren and have had no child, but you are going to conceive and give birth to a son.

> 4 From now on, take great care. Drink no wine or fermented liquor, and eat nothing unclean.

> 5 For you are going to conceive and give birth to a son. No razor is to touch his head, for the boy is to be God's nazirite from his mother's womb; and he will start rescuing Israel from the power of the Philistines.'

> 6 The woman then went and told her husband, 'A man of God has just come to me, who looked like the Angel of God, so majestic was he. I did not ask him where he came from, and he did not tell me his name.

> 7 But he said to me, "You are going to conceive and will give birth to a son. From now on, drink no wine or fermented liquor, and eat nothing unclean. For the boy is to be God's nazirite from his mother's womb to his dying day." '

> 8 Manoah then pleaded with [the LORD] and said, 'I beg you, Lord, let the man of God that you sent come to us again and instruct us what to do about the child when he is born.'

> 9 [the LORD] heard Manoah's prayer, and the Angel of [the LORD] visited the woman again while she was sitting in a field and when her husband Manoah was not with her.

> 10 The woman quickly ran and told her husband, 'Look,' she said, 'the man who came to me the other day has appeared to me again.'

> 11 Manoah got up, followed his wife, came to the man and said to him, 'Are you the man who spoke to this woman?' He replied, 'I am.'

> 12 Manoah then said, 'When your words come true, what will be the boy's way of life?'

> 13 The Angel of [the LORD] replied to Manoah, 'From everything that I forbade this woman, let her abstain.

> 14 Let her swallow nothing that comes from the vine, let her drink no wine or fermented liquor, let her eat nothing unclean and let her obey all the orders that I have given her.'

> 15 Manoah then said to the Angel of [the LORD], 'Allow us to detain you while we prepare a kid for you' -- for Manoah did not know that this was the Angel of [the LORD].

> 16 The Angel of [the LORD] said to Manoah, 'Even if you did detain me, I should not eat your food; but if you wish to prepare a burnt offering, offer it to [the LORD].'

> 17 Manoah then said to the Angel of [the LORD], 'What is your name, so that we may honour you when your words come true?'

> 18 The Angel of [the LORD] replied, 'Why ask my name? It is a name of wonder.'

> 19 Manoah then took the kid and the oblation and offered it on the rock as a burnt offering to [the LORD] the Wonderworker. Manoah and his wife looked on.

> 20 Now, as the flame rose heavenwards from the altar, the Angel of [the LORD] ascended in this flame before the eyes of Manoah and his wife, and they fell face downwards on the ground.

> 21 After this, the Angel of [the LORD] did not appear any more to Manoah and his wife, but Manoah understood that this had been the Angel of [the LORD].

> 22 And Manoah said to his wife, 'We are certain to die, because we have seen God.'

> 23 His wife replied, 'If [the LORD] had meant to kill us, he would not have accepted a burnt offering and oblation from us, he would not have let us see all this and, at the same time, have told us such things.'

[Judges 13.2-23 NJB](https://www.crosswire.org/study/parallelstudy.jsp?key=Judges+13%3A2#cv)

Here we see the holiness aspect. Moses was told to take his sandals off because he was approaching holy ground. Holiness is dedication to God, being reserved for his use. Samson's mother is told that Samson will be a Nazirite, just like John the Baptist in the New Testament. He was to be dedicated to God, his life would be for God.

Just as in Hagar's case, God's Angel talks to a woman. We don't know her name but God does. He could have talked to Manoah but he chooses to talk to her instead. Manoah said, *We're done for*, but she said *Listen dummy, if God was going to kill us he wouldn't have accepted anything from us, would he?* (That's my paraphrase.) There is a theme that runs through the entire Bible and it's clear to see: God is for women.

Getting back to Manoah, he wants to know the Angel's name. As with Jacob, the Angel answers this question with another question: *Why ask my name?* but on this occasion adds something more: *It is a name of wonder.*

When Moses asks what name he should use when the Israelites ask for it, God gives an enigmatic answer: *I am who I am.* Given the way Hebrew works, this can also be translated as *I will be who I will be*, or *I exist*, or even, *I'm not saying who I am.* To Manoah the Angel says his name is wonderful. A longer explanation of the meaning of the *wonderful* word is, *state in which an event is out of the ordinary, inspiring amazement, and usually associated with the supernatural*. The only other place that particular word is used in the Hebrew Bible is in Psalm 139 and verse 6:

> Such knowledge is too wonderful for me; it is high, I cannot attain it.

One last thing. Here, as in other places where God's Angel appears, the story starts with the Angel speaking and ends with God speaking. It's hard to tell whether it's the Angel or God.

## So what?

So what has all of this got to do with anything? For me, these accounts resonate with certain places in the New Testament. One place is when Jesus says, *before Abraham was, I AM.* [John 8.58] Another place is when Philip asked Jesus, *Lord, let us see the Father and then we shall be satisfied*, to which Jesus responds, *Have I been with you all this time, Philip, and you still do not know me?* [John 14.9]. Yet another place is when Jesus appeared to Thomas after the resurrection:

> 24 Thomas, called the Twin, who was one of the Twelve, was not with them when Jesus came.

> 25 So the other disciples said to him, 'We have seen the Lord,' but he answered, 'Unless I can see the holes that the nails made in his hands and can put my finger into the holes they made, and unless I can put my hand into his side, I refuse to believe.'

> 26 Eight days later the disciples were in the house again and Thomas was with them. The doors were closed, but Jesus came in and stood among them. 'Peace be with you,' he said.

> 27 Then he spoke to Thomas, 'Put your finger here; look, here are my hands. Give me your hand; put it into my side. Do not be unbelieving any more but believe.'

> 28 Thomas replied, 'My Lord and my God!'

In these places and others as well, the people who wrote the New Testament make clear that Jesus is more than just a messenger of God but is in fact God himself.

## One last place

To finish I'd like to look at one more place in the New Testament. Jesus was sitting at a well when a Samaritan woman came up. There is the trouble. She was a Samaritan, a people who at the time were regarded as outcasts. The fact that she went to the well at midday means that she was an outcast among outcasts: the time to go to a well is early in the morning before it gets too hot. It seems that she was not welcome there with the other women of her village, the ones who got the water every day as women do.

Of great surprise, given that Jews and Samaritans did not talk, Jesus struck up a conversation with her. If you think of Jesus as God's wonderful messenger then there he is, waiting for her. He gives her a hard job, *Give me a drink of water*, which means lowering the bucket twice. He gives her a promise: *If you ask me for it, I will give you running water*, by which he means God's Spirit. Then he gives her a harder job: *Go and call your husband*. This is hard because she hasn't got one at the moment.

As they speak she has a growing realisation that he is not an ordinary person. She realises he is a prophet -- someone who speaks for God-- when he lets her know that he knows all about herself, things that only God can know. She begins to wonder whether he could be the promised *Messiah*, the great emancipator himself. When she runs to tell the people in her village she names him: *Come and see a man who has told me everything I ever did. I wonder if he is the Christ?*

So far so good, but there are some things that don't match up. Where is the dedication to God or the fear of death after seeing the Living One? We don't know her name from the Bible but the people of the Eastern Orthodox Church know her name: Photini, which means *Little Light*. According to them, she became a follower of Christ, received the name Photini in baptism, proclaimed the Gospel over a wide area, and was later martyred. According to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritan_woman_at_the_well#Veneration):

> Her continuing witness is said to have brought so many to the Christian faith that she is described as "equal to the apostles". Eventually, having drawn the attention of Emperor Nero, she was brought before him to answer for her faith, suffering many tortures and dying a martyr after being thrown down a dry well.

So she *was* dedicated to God. There is a sense in which people who see God's Wonderful Messenger do die. It is a death of the human nature but life in the Spirit, the running water which Christ gives. A human tyrant can have that person thrown down a dry well. However, though they are weak, the One who sent them is strong and is with them yet.

Amen
